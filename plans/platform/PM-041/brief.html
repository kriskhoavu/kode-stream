The header band becomes a choice

Kode Stream paints a decorative band below the topbar on three routes, and today it paints exactly one thing: a field of 29 drifting neon icons, hardcoded with no way to change or turn it off. PM-041 adds a second treatment, Lattice, makes it the default, keeps Neon, and puts the choice in Settings.

The short version

One treatment, wired straight into the shell

The neon field arrived as a single component that owns everything at once: the band geometry, the route list, the mask, and the 29 icons with their per-icon hues and animated glow filters. Nothing separates what the band is from what it shows, so there is no seam at which a second treatment could exist and no switch anyone could reach.

After the plan the band contract is unchanged — same three routes, same stacking against the sticky left nav — but its contents come from a variant chosen in Settings, and it settles at one 240px height instead of three per-route ones. Lattice becomes the default, Neon stays one click away, and a third option turns the band off and gives the topbar back its own fill, blur and border.

Figure 1

The change is a seam, not a new backdrop

Adding Lattice beside Neon is the small half of this ticket. The load-bearing part is splitting the one module into a band that any variant can fill and variants that know nothing about the band.

Figure 2

Turning the band off is not the same as hiding it

The .header-band class does more than position the backdrop: it strips the topbar's background, its blur and its bottom border, and it drops the sticky left nav to z-index: 0 so the backdrop can paint across it. Rendering an empty band would therefore leave a topbar with no chrome and nothing behind it. One condition has to drive both the class and the render.

Figure 3

The lattice is generated, and four properties are what make it a lattice

Neon's placements are a literal array — 29 lines anyone can read in a diff. Lattice cannot work that way, because its meaning is in the walk structure and a flat list of coordinates hides exactly that. So it is generated from a seed, once at module load, and the properties a careless edit would break are asserted instead.

Figure 4

Three layers move on two periods, and that is the whole effect

Stacking a grid and a node network gives you two decorations. They become one field only because the network travels exactly as far as the grid does, in exactly the same time, and because a single light pass crosses both on its own shared beat.

Delivery

Twelve phases, and only one of them is the new backdrop

Five planned and seven added after — one of them wrong, left visible rather than tidied away, and one a bug the plan never anticipated. F1 and F2 are the seam. They landed before Lattice existed, and each was verifiable on its own terms: F1 by its fallback behaviour, F2 by Neon looking identical afterwards. One correction during delivery — F1 keeps the default at neon and F3 flips it, because defaulting to a variant that does not exist yet would have left the band blank for two phases.

F1

Preference and variant type

done

Establishes the variant union and the stored preference, with no UI attached. The fallback is the whole phase: every existing install reaches this code with the field absent, and a normalizer that throws or returns undefined blanks the band with no error anyone would notice.

F2

Band contract generalisation

done

Moves the band, the route list and the mask out of the neon module and renames the neon-specific classes and variables. Nothing should look different afterwards, which is what makes it reviewable separately from F3.

  • The existing neon tests are retargeted, not rewritten — keeping their assertions is the evidence the rename changed names only.
F3

Lattice variant

done

The new treatment, behind the dispatcher F2 built: the seeded walk generator, the grid and network styling, and the two shared motion periods. Its acceptance criteria are the four geometric invariants in Figure 3.

F4

Settings control

done

A radio group in an Appearance section — three mutually exclusive options, unlike the checkbox rows above it. Copy carries real weight here, because Settings is not a backdrop route and the control has no live preview to explain itself.

F5

Cross-variant verification

done

The join, and a phase rather than a checklist item because no unit test answers whether the result is legible. Two variants across two themes and three routes, plus off, plus reduced motion, plus keyboard focus over both fields.

F6

Monochrome lattice palette

reverted

A misdiagnosis, kept on the record. The field read as orange, and --blue being #f9b98c in the dark palette looked like explanation enough, so the lattice was redrawn in --text. Wrong layer: its own blue was working, including in light theme where this threw it away.

F7

Flat ground under the lattice

done

The orange was .main-content's two corner glows behind the band, reading from that same token. They suit Neon, whose icons are themselves soft points of colour; a thin, even, structural mark on an uneven wash just looks unevenly lit. The shell now names the active variant so the page beneath can drop them.

F8

One band height

done

The band ran 300 to 430px by route, inherited from the neon field's need to clear each page's content. It read as too tall, and the variation read as arbitrary. One height everywhere now; the mask fades the lower third to nothing at any height, so the extra was paint area with nothing visible in it. .header-band-{route} went with it — nothing consumed it.

F9

Topbar popups above content

done

A reported bug, and one the band caused from the start. Giving the topbar a z-index makes it a stacking context, so a popup inside is capped at the topbar's rank however high it asks — the workspace menu asks for 35, gets 2, ties with page content and loses on DOM order. It lost the clicks too, not just the paint. The topbar moves to 3.

F10

Lit lattice junctions

done

The nodes were flat fills, so the sweep passing over them had nothing to catch and the field read as drawn rather than lit. Every node takes a halo — hung on one group, not sixty circles — and coarse junctions add a brighter one breathing on the sweep's own period.

F11

Nav edge on the panel border

done

The left nav drew its right edge from a darkened --nav, invisible against a near-black nav in dark theme, so the nav and the page merged wherever the band crossed between them. It reads from --line now, like every other panel in the app.

F12

Band height to 240px

done

F8 had cut a little too far: at 200px the fade landed through the filter row rather than below it. 240px is ten whole lattice cells, so the grid ends on a rule.

A z-index inside the band is not what it says

The band gives the topbar and the page content their own ranks, which makes each a stacking context. Any popup inside either one is capped at its parent's rank, so raising a menu's own z-index does nothing. Order the containers, not the popups — and verify with a hit test, because a popup can be fully painted and still be losing its clicks.

The rename is not cosmetic

A route class reading neon-band-canvas while the band paints a lattice is a lie the next reader has to decode. It is also the class that controls the topbar's chrome, so leaving it under a variant name buries the coupling Figure 2 exists to make visible.

Scope

Deliberately not included

  • Per-route variants. One setting covers the whole app. A per-route choice would need the preference to become a map and the Settings control to become a table, for a distinction nobody has asked to draw.
  • New backdrop routes. backdropRoutes stays exactly Workstream, Workbench and Knowledge. Widening it is a separate decision about which pages carry decoration, not about which decoration they carry.
  • Colour or density controls. Both variants read existing theme tokens. A control here would mean new tokens and a contrast question on every combination.
  • Syncing the choice across browsers. It is a local preference, like theme, and would come back in only if app preferences as a whole move server-side.

Open

2 decisions still to make

Which seed produces the field we actually ship?

The plan fixes the generator, not the constant it starts from. Different seeds give visibly different coverage — some leave the left nav bare, some crowd the page title. Options: pick one at F3 review and freeze it; or generate a handful and choose from screenshots, which costs one extra review round. Khoa decides at F3.

Does the Settings control need a preview?

Settings is not a backdrop route, so choosing a variant shows no immediate change — the user has to navigate away to see what they picked. Options: ship with descriptive copy only, as planned; add a small static thumbnail per option, which means maintaining a second rendering of each variant; or paint the band on Settings too, which contradicts the scope decision above. Khoa decides at F4.
